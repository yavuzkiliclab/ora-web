Aesthetic Dentistry

Aesthetic dentistry brings together treatments that improve the appearance of your smile, such as veneers, bonding, and whitening. These are often planned together as part of a smile design, where the shape, shade, and alignment of the teeth are considered as a whole, so that the result looks natural and suits your face.

Who it may suit

Aesthetic treatments are often considered by people who would like to change the colour, shape, or spacing of their teeth, or who want to refresh a smile that has worn or discoloured over time. What is suitable depends on the condition of your teeth and gums, your expectations, and whether any underlying dental health needs to be addressed first. This is decided by your dentist after examining your case, so the notes here are general rather than a recommendation for any individual.

How it usually works

  1. Smile design. Your dentist plans the result, often on screen, and you review and approve the proposed look before treatment begins.
  2. Preparation. Depending on the option chosen, teeth may be lightly prepared, or treatment may be minimally invasive.
  3. Placement or application. Veneers are fitted, or bonding and whitening are carried out, according to your plan.
  4. Review. The result is checked with you and adjusted where needed.

What to expect

Aesthetic treatments are often completed within a short visit of several days, though this depends on the specific treatment and your case. Some options are reversible or minimally invasive, while others involve preparing the teeth, and your dentist will explain the differences. Your dentist will describe what is realistic for your situation, and results vary from person to person.

Recovery and aftercare

Most aesthetic treatments involve little or no downtime, though some sensitivity can be normal for a short time, particularly after whitening. Your dentist will give you specific guidance on caring for your new smile, which usually includes good daily oral hygiene and avoiding habits that can stain or damage the teeth. Regular check-ups help keep the result looking its best over time.

Common questions about aesthetic dentistry

What is the difference between veneers and bonding?

Both can improve the appearance of teeth, but they differ in materials, durability, and how the teeth are prepared. Your dentist will explain which option suits your case after examining you.

Will my smile look natural?

Smile design aims for a result that suits your face and looks natural. You review and approve the proposed look before treatment begins, so you know what to expect.

How long do aesthetic treatments last?

This depends on the treatment, the materials, and how you care for your teeth. Your dentist can explain what supports longevity in your case. There is no single answer that applies to everyone.

Does whitening damage the teeth?

Professional whitening is carried out under a dentist's guidance. Some temporary sensitivity can be normal. Your dentist will assess whether it is suitable for you.

Related treatments

Aesthetic dentistry is often planned alongside other care. Where teeth need strengthening as well as reshaping, crowns and bridges may be part of the plan. Where alignment is the main concern, orthodontics may be considered first. Healthy gums, cared for through periodontology, are the foundation of an attractive smile.
