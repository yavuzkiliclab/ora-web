Periodontology

Periodontology is the care of the gums and the tissues that support the teeth. Healthy gums are the foundation that every implant, crown, and veneer depends on, so gum health is often addressed before or alongside other treatments to give lasting results the best chance.

Who it may suit

Periodontal care may be considered by people with gum inflammation, bleeding gums, bad breath, or diagnosed gum disease, or as part of preparing for other treatments. What is suitable is determined by your dentist after examining the condition of your gums and the supporting tissues.

How it usually works

  1. Assessment. Your dentist examines the health of your gums and the tissues that support the teeth.
  2. Treatment. Cleaning and gum treatments are carried out according to your needs.
  3. Guidance. You are given advice on maintaining gum health at home.
  4. Review. Progress is checked and further care planned where needed.

What to expect

Gum treatment depends on the condition being addressed, and your dentist will explain the approach for your case. Some sensitivity can be normal after treatment. Maintaining gum health at home is an important part of the result, and outcomes vary from person to person.

Recovery and aftercare

Good daily oral hygiene is the most important part of caring for your gums after treatment. Your dentist will explain how to clean effectively and gently, and may recommend regular maintenance visits. Because gum health underpins so much other dental work, keeping up with aftercare helps protect implants, crowns, and veneers over time.

Common questions about periodontology

What are the signs of gum problems?

Bleeding gums, redness, swelling, bad breath, or gum recession can be signs of gum problems. If you notice these, a dentist can assess what is happening. Only an examination can confirm the cause.

Why do healthy gums matter for other treatments?

Gums and the tissues beneath them support the teeth, implants, crowns, and veneers. Treating gum problems first gives other dental work a stronger foundation.

Is gum treatment uncomfortable?

The approach depends on the condition being treated, and your dentist will discuss comfort with you. Some sensitivity afterwards can be normal.

Can gum disease be reversed?

This depends on the type and stage of the condition, which your dentist assesses. Early care and good daily hygiene play an important role.

Related treatments

Gum health supports the rest of your dental care. Healthy gums are essential before a dental implant or a crown or bridge, and they are the foundation of an attractive result from aesthetic dentistry.
