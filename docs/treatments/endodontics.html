Endodontics

Endodontics, commonly known as root canal treatment, treats infection or damage inside a tooth. It aims to save a tooth that might otherwise need to be removed, by cleaning the inside of the tooth, disinfecting it, and sealing it so it can continue to function.

Who it may suit

Root canal treatment may be considered when the inside of a tooth is infected or inflamed, which often causes pain, sensitivity to hot or cold, or swelling. Whether it is needed and suitable is determined by your dentist after examination and imaging.

How it usually works

  1. Assessment. Your dentist examines the tooth and uses imaging to understand the problem.
  2. Treatment. The inside of the tooth is cleaned and shaped under local anaesthesia.
  3. Sealing. The cleaned space is filled and sealed.
  4. Restoration. The tooth is often restored with a crown or filling to protect it.

What to expect

Modern root canal treatment is usually carried out with local anaesthesia for comfort, and is often more straightforward than its reputation suggests. Timing and the number of visits depend on your case, and your dentist will explain what to expect. Recovery varies from person to person.

Recovery and aftercare

Some tenderness can be normal for a short time after root canal treatment. Your dentist will give you guidance on caring for the tooth, which usually includes good daily oral hygiene and being careful with the tooth until any final restoration is in place. Because a treated tooth is often protected with a crown, following through with the recommended restoration helps support the long-term result.

Common questions about root canal treatment

Is root canal treatment painful?

Modern root canal treatment is usually carried out under local anaesthesia for comfort. Your dentist will discuss what to expect with you. Experiences vary from person to person.

How many visits does it take?

This depends on your case. Some treatments are completed in a single visit, others need more. Your dentist will explain after examining the tooth.

Will I need a crown afterwards?

A treated tooth is often restored with a crown to protect it, but whether this applies to you is determined by your dentist.

Can the tooth be saved?

Root canal treatment aims to save a tooth that might otherwise be lost, but whether it is the right option depends on the condition of the tooth, which your dentist assesses.

Related treatments

Root canal treatment is often part of a wider plan. A treated tooth is frequently protected afterwards with a crown. If a tooth cannot be saved, your dentist may discuss removal through oral surgery and replacement with a dental implant.
