Dental Implants

A dental implant is a small titanium post placed in the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. Once healed, it supports a crown, bridge, or denture that looks and functions like a natural tooth. Implants are used for anything from a single missing tooth to full-arch restoration, and they are designed to be a long-lasting way to restore both the appearance and the function of your smile.

Who implants may suit

Implants are often considered by people who have lost one or more teeth and would like a stable, long-lasting replacement that does not rely on the neighbouring teeth for support. They can be an option for a single gap, several missing teeth, or a full arch. Whether implants are right for you depends on your oral health, the amount and quality of bone in your jaw, your gum health, and your general medical history. This can only be determined by a dentist after examining your case, so the notes here are general rather than a recommendation for any individual.

How the treatment usually works

Every case is planned individually, but the general path is as follows:

  1. Consultation and imaging. Your dentist reviews your case, and 3D imaging is used to assess the bone and plan the position of the implant precisely.
  2. Placement. The implant post is placed into the jawbone in the planned position, usually under local anaesthesia.
  3. Healing. A healing period allows the implant to integrate with the bone. The length of this period varies from person to person.
  4. The final restoration. Once healing is complete, a custom crown, bridge, or denture is made and fitted onto the implant.

What to expect

Implant treatment is usually planned over more than one stage, often across two visits with a healing period in between. The exact timing depends on your individual case, whether any preparatory work such as bone grafting is needed, and how your body heals. Your dentist confirms the plan after examination. Comfort, recovery, and results vary from person to person, and your dentist will explain what is realistic for your situation.

Recovery and aftercare

After an implant is placed, it is normal to need a short period of care while the area settles. Your dentist will give you specific instructions, which usually include keeping the area clean, eating softer foods for a time, and following any guidance on activity. Good day-to-day oral hygiene and regular check-ups help support the long-term health of an implant. If you are travelling from abroad, aftercare can be coordinated remotely once you return home, and you should follow your dentist's advice on what to watch for during healing.

Common questions about implants

How long does implant treatment take?

It depends on your case. Implants are often planned over two visits with a healing period in between, but the exact timing is confirmed by your dentist after examining you. Some cases are more straightforward than others.

Is the procedure painful?

Implant placement is usually carried out under local anaesthesia. Your dentist will discuss comfort with you and explain what to expect during and after the procedure. Experiences differ from person to person.

How long do implants last?

Implants are designed to be a long-lasting solution, but how long they last depends on factors such as your oral hygiene, gum health, and regular check-ups. Your dentist can explain what supports the longevity of an implant in your case.

What if I do not have enough bone?

In some cases, a preparatory procedure such as bone grafting may be considered before an implant. Whether this is needed is assessed with imaging and determined by your dentist.

Related treatments

Implant treatment is sometimes planned alongside other care. If the jaw needs preparation, oral surgery may be part of the plan. The final restoration on an implant is often a crown or bridge. Healthy gums are the foundation for any implant, which is where periodontology comes in.
