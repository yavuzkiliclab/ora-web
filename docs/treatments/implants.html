Dental Implants

A dental implant is a small titanium post placed in the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. Once healed, it supports a crown, bridge, or denture that looks and functions like a natural tooth. Implants are used for anything from a single missing tooth to full-arch restoration.

Who implants may suit

Implants are often considered by people who have lost one or more teeth and would like a stable, long-lasting replacement. Whether implants are right for you depends on your oral health, bone condition, and medical history. This can only be determined by a dentist after examining your case, so the notes here are general rather than a recommendation for any individual.

How the treatment usually works

Every case is planned individually, but the general path is as follows:

  1. Consultation and imaging. Your dentist reviews your case, and 3D imaging is used to assess bone and plan placement precisely.
  2. Placement. The implant post is placed into the jawbone in a planned position.
  3. Healing. A healing period allows the implant to integrate with the bone. The length varies from person to person.
  4. The final restoration. Once healing is complete, a custom crown, bridge, or denture is fitted onto the implant.

What to expect

Implant treatment is usually planned over more than one stage, often across two visits with a healing period in between. Exact timing depends on your individual case and is confirmed by your dentist after examination. Comfort, recovery, and results vary from person to person, and your dentist will explain what is realistic for your situation.
