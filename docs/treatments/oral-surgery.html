Oral Surgery

Oral surgery covers procedures such as tooth extractions, the removal of impacted wisdom teeth, and bone procedures that prepare the jaw for implants. These are carried out to relieve problems, remove teeth that cannot be saved, or create the right conditions for further treatment.

Who it may suit

Oral surgery may be considered when a tooth cannot be saved, when wisdom teeth are impacted or causing problems, or when the jawbone needs to be prepared before implants. Whether a procedure is needed and suitable is determined by your dentist after examination and imaging, taking your general health into account.

How it usually works

  1. Assessment and imaging. Your dentist reviews your case with 3D imaging where needed to plan the procedure carefully.
  2. The procedure. The extraction, wisdom tooth removal, or bone procedure is carried out under appropriate anaesthesia.
  3. Healing guidance. You are given clear aftercare instructions to support recovery.
  4. Follow-up. Healing is checked, and any next steps, such as an implant, are planned.

What to expect

Recovery varies depending on the procedure and your case. Some swelling or discomfort can be normal for a few days, and your dentist will explain what to expect and how to care for the area afterwards. Comfort and recovery differ from person to person.

Recovery and aftercare

After oral surgery, following your dentist's aftercare instructions carefully supports healing. This usually includes resting the area, eating softer foods for a time, keeping the area clean as advised, and following any guidance on activity. Your dentist will tell you what to watch for and when to seek advice. If you are travelling from abroad, aftercare can be coordinated remotely once you return home.

Common questions about oral surgery

Will the procedure be painful?

Oral surgery is carried out under appropriate anaesthesia, and your dentist will discuss comfort with you. Some discomfort during healing can be normal, and you will be given guidance on managing it.

How long does recovery take?

Recovery depends on the procedure and your case. Your dentist will explain what to expect for your situation. Healing varies from person to person.

Why do wisdom teeth need to be removed?

Wisdom teeth are sometimes removed when they are impacted or causing problems, but not always. Whether removal is needed is determined by your dentist after examination and imaging.

Is bone grafting always needed before an implant?

No. Bone grafting is only considered when there is not enough bone to support an implant, and whether it is needed is assessed with imaging by your dentist.

Related treatments

Oral surgery often prepares the way for other treatments. Bone procedures may be carried out before a dental implant. Where a tooth is being removed, your dentist may discuss replacing it with a bridge or an implant. Gum health, cared for through periodontology, supports healing.
